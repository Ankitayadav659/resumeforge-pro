Resume for freshers

Resume for freshers: the format, sections and examples that get shortlisted

Writing your first job resume is hard when you have little work experience. The good news: recruiters hiring freshers are not looking for experience. They look for proof that you can learn, build and deliver. Here is how to show it on one page.

The best fresher resume format

Use a single-column, reverse-chronological format on one page. Single column because applicant tracking systems read it reliably. Reverse-chronological because recruiters expect your most recent and most relevant work first. One page because, as a fresher, a tight page with strong content beats two pages of filler.

Skip the photo, date of birth, marital status, father's name, religion and full home address. None of these help you get shortlisted, they take space from your achievements, and many employers prefer not to see them. City and state are enough for location.

Section order for a fresher resume

Put what is strongest first. For most freshers, that means education and projects come before any experience:

  1. Header: name, phone, professional email, city, LinkedIn and GitHub or portfolio (if relevant).
  2. Headline: the role you want, for example "Computer Science Graduate | Java, Spring Boot, SQL".
  3. Summary: two or three lines on what you studied, what you have built and the role you are targeting.
  4. Education: degree, college, graduation year and CGPA or percentage.
  5. Projects: two to four projects with what you built, the tools you used and the outcome.
  6. Internships / experience: including part-time work, freelance work and college roles with real responsibility.
  7. Skills: grouped (Languages, Tools, Frameworks), only skills you can answer questions about.
  8. Certifications and achievements: hackathons, competitions, scholarships, ranks.

What to include in each section

Education and CGPA

List your degree, specialisation, college, university and year. Include your CGPA if it is 7.0/10 or above, or your percentage if it is 70% or above. Many campus placement drives have cut-offs, so a strong score is worth showing. If your score is lower, leave it out and let projects carry the section, unless the application form asks for it. Class XII and X marks are optional; add them only if they are strong or the employer asks.

Projects with outcomes

Projects are where freshers prove skill. For each one, say what you built, how you built it and what happened as a result: accuracy, users, speed, a grade, a prize or a deployment. Link to the code or a live demo if you can.

Internships

Treat internships like real jobs. Start each bullet with an action verb and say what changed because of your work. Even a two-month internship can produce one or two bullets with numbers.

Achievements with numbers

"Participated in a hackathon" is weak. "Placed 3rd of 120 teams at [hackathon name]" is strong. Numbers give recruiters a sense of scale: team size, number of participants, rank, funds raised, people trained.

Sample fresher resume bullets: before and after

Here is how weak, duty-style bullets become achievement bullets. Square brackets show where you would put your own real numbers.

Before: Made a website for college fest.
After: Built the college fest website with React and Firebase, handling [1,200] registrations and online payments in [3] weeks.
Before: Did internship at a marketing company.
After: Wrote and scheduled [24] Instagram posts during a 2-month internship, increasing average engagement by [18%].
Before: Worked on machine learning project.
After: Trained a crop disease classifier in Python (TensorFlow) on [5,000] leaf images, reaching [92%] test accuracy.
Before: Was part of the placement committee.
After: Coordinated [15] company visits as placement committee member, scheduling tests and interviews for [300+] students.

Common fresher resume mistakes

  • An objective that is about you: "Seeking a challenging role to grow my skills" says nothing. Say what you offer instead.
  • Listing every course and tool: a long skills list without proof looks padded. Keep skills you can defend in an interview.
  • Duties without results: "Responsible for testing" should become "Tested [40] API endpoints and logged [12] defects fixed before release".
  • Unprofessional email: use a simple firstname.lastname style address.
  • Fancy two-column templates: they often break in ATS software. Read why in our ATS-friendly resume guide.
  • Typos and inconsistent dates: a single typo can cost you at a high-volume campus drive. Proofread and keep one date format.
  • The same resume for every job: mirror the keywords in each job description. Our ATS resume checker shows which ones you are missing.

Tailor it for campus placements and job portals

For campus placements, keep a master resume and adjust the headline, summary and project order for each company. A product company will care most about your projects and coding skills; a consulting firm will look for analytical work, leadership in college societies and communication. For portals like Naukri and company career pages, export a clean PDF with real text, and keep a plain-text copy for forms that ask you to paste your resume.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best resume format for freshers?

A single-column, reverse-chronological format on one page. Put education and projects before experience, and use standard section headings so applicant tracking systems can read it.

Should freshers mention CGPA on their resume?

Yes, if it is 7.0/10 or above, or 70% or above. If it is lower, you can leave it out and let your projects and internships carry the section, unless the employer asks for it.

Should I add a photo, date of birth or marital status?

No. These details do not help you get shortlisted and take space away from your skills and achievements. Your name, phone, email, city and LinkedIn are enough.

How long should a fresher resume be?

One page. Recruiters screening many fresher applications want to see your education, best projects and skills quickly.

What if I have no internship?

Lead with projects, college roles, freelance work, competitions and certifications. Describe each with an action verb and a measurable outcome, just as you would a job.
