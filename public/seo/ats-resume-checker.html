What is an ATS, and why does your resume need a check?

An applicant tracking system (ATS) is the software companies use to collect, store and search job applications. Workday, Taleo, Greenhouse, Lever and Naukri RMS are common examples. When you apply online, the ATS reads your resume, pulls out your name, contact details, job titles, dates, skills and education, and stores them so recruiters can search and filter candidates.

Two things can go wrong. First, the ATS may not read your resume properly if it uses columns, tables, text boxes or an image-based PDF. Second, even when it reads the file correctly, a recruiter searching for "SQL" or "stakeholder management" will not find you if those words are not on your resume. An ATS resume checker catches both problems before you click Apply.

Passing the software is only half the job. A human still reads every resume that gets shortlisted, usually in a few seconds. That is why the ResumeForge Pro checker scores your resume the way a recruiter would, not only the way a parser would.

The 14 checks behind your resume score

The Recruiter Score updates live while you edit. These are the 14 points it audits:

  1. Contact details: name, email, phone and location are present and easy to find.
  2. Headline: a clear target title under your name, such as "Data Analyst | SQL, Power BI, Python".
  3. Summary length and clichés: a summary of sensible length, without filler like "hard-working team player".
  4. Action-verb bullets: bullets that start with a strong verb (Led, Built, Reduced) instead of "Responsible for".
  5. Quantified impact: the share of bullets that include a number, percentage, amount or time saved.
  6. Bullet length: bullets long enough to show a result, short enough to scan (roughly one to two lines).
  7. Weak phrases and clichés: flags phrases such as "worked on", "helped with" and "various tasks".
  8. Pronouns: resumes are written without "I", "me" or "my".
  9. Bullets per role: enough bullets to show what you did, without burying the best ones.
  10. Complete dates: every role and degree has start and end dates in a consistent format.
  11. Skills count: a focused skills section, neither empty nor a wall of 60 tools.
  12. Page length: one page for freshers and anyone with under five years of experience, two for experienced professionals, up to three for executives.
  13. Level-specific essentials: the sections that matter for your career mode, such as projects and education for freshers or career highlights for executives.
  14. Industry fit: extra checks for General, Tech, Consulting or Finance roles, so the advice matches what recruiters in that field expect.

Because the checks adapt to your career mode (Fresher, Experienced Professional or Senior-Executive), a final-year student is not penalised for having no work history, and a director is not told to add their CGPA.

Job description keyword matching

A general score tells you whether your resume is well written. A keyword match tells you whether it is written for this job. Paste the job description into the editor and ResumeForge Pro lists the important keywords it found, split into those your resume already contains and those it is missing.

Aim for a match of 70% or more. You do not need every keyword: some will be things you genuinely have not done, and you should never claim skills you cannot discuss in an interview. Focus on the missing keywords that are true for you and add them where they belong, in your skills section and inside the bullets that prove them.

Keyword missing: The job asks for "stakeholder management", but your bullet says "Coordinated with other teams on the release".
Keyword matched: Led stakeholder management across 4 teams for a quarterly release, cutting sign-off time from [10] to [6] days.

If you want help, the AI tailoring tool reads the job description and your resume together, then suggests a tailored headline, summary and reworded bullets in the employer's language. It never invents experience or numbers; where a figure would help, it leaves a [placeholder] for you to fill in.

Beyond the score: the recruiter's 6-second scan

Recruiters skim before they read. The 6-second scan panel shows the details that stand out in that first look: your name and headline, your most recent title and employer, and the numbers in your top bullets. If your best achievement is hidden in the fourth bullet of your second job, move it up.
