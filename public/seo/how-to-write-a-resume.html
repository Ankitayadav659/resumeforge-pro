How to write a resume: a step-by-step guide with examples

A resume has one job: to get you an interview. This guide walks through every section in the order you should write it, with before-and-after examples, so you finish with a resume that is clear, specific and tailored to the role you want.

1. Choose your level and format

Before you write a word, decide which stage of your career you are writing for. It changes what goes first and what recruiters expect to see.

  • Fresher or student: education and projects lead, followed by internships, skills and achievements. See our resume guide for freshers.
  • Experienced professional: experience leads, with your most recent role carrying the most detail and the strongest numbers.
  • Senior leader or executive: an executive profile and career highlights come first, so the scope of your work (team size, budget, revenue, markets) is clear in seconds.

For the format, use a single-column, reverse-chronological layout: most recent first, one column, standard headings such as Experience, Education and Skills. It is what recruiters expect and what applicant tracking systems (ATS) read most reliably. Our ATS-friendly resume templates all follow this structure.

2. Contact details and headline

At the top, include your full name, phone number, a professional email address, your city and state (or city and country), and a LinkedIn URL. Add GitHub, a portfolio or publications only if they are relevant to the role. You do not need a full street address, a photo, your date of birth or your marital status.

Directly under your name, add a headline: the job title you want, plus two or three of your strongest skills or credentials. It tells the recruiter in one line what you are and what you are applying for.

3. Write a short summary

The summary is three or four lines that answer: who are you professionally, what are you best at, and what have you achieved? Write it last if that is easier, once you know your strongest material. Avoid clichés like "hard-working", "go-getter" and "team player". They take up space and prove nothing.

Before: Hard-working and dedicated professional seeking a challenging role in a reputed organisation where I can grow my skills.
After: Financial analyst with 4 years in FP&A at a consumer goods company. Built the monthly forecasting model used by 6 business units and reduced forecast variance by [X%]. Looking to bring planning and modelling skills to a fintech finance team.

Notice the "after" version has no "I". Resumes are written in an implied first person, without pronouns.

4. Experience bullets that show results

This is the section that decides most shortlists. For each role, list your job title, employer, location and dates, then three to six bullets. Each bullet should follow a simple formula:

Action verb + what you did + measurable result. Start with a strong verb, describe the work in specific terms, and end with the outcome: a number, a percentage, money, time saved, or scale.

A useful variation is the X-Y-Z formula: accomplished X, measured by Y, by doing Z. It forces you to state the result first, then prove it, then explain how.

Before: Responsible for handling customer complaints.
After: Resolved [40+] customer complaints a week, raising the team's satisfaction score from [3.8] to [4.5] by introducing a same-day callback rule.
Before: Worked on the company's data pipeline.
After: Cut daily report generation time from 3 hours to 20 minutes by rebuilding the ETL pipeline in Python and Airflow.
Before: Helped the sales team with presentations.
After: Built pitch decks for [12] enterprise prospects, contributing to [4] closed deals worth ₹[X] lakh.

A few rules make bullets stronger:

  • Lead with your best bullet in each role. Recruiters often read only the first one or two.
  • Use numbers honestly. If you do not have an exact figure, use a defensible estimate ("about 30%") or a measure of scale ("for 200 users"). Never invent one; interviewers will ask.
  • Keep bullets to one or two lines. If a bullet runs to three lines, split it or cut it.
  • Avoid weak openers such as "Responsible for", "Worked on", "Helped with" and "Involved in". Our list of resume action verbs has 150+ replacements.
  • Use past tense for past roles and present tense for ongoing work in your current role.

5. Skills and keywords

Your skills section helps both the ATS and the recruiter. Group skills so they are easy to scan, for example:

  • Languages: Python, SQL, JavaScript
  • Tools: Power BI, Tableau, Jira, Git
  • Domain: Financial modelling, Forecasting, Variance analysis

Take your keywords from the job description. If it says "stakeholder management" and you have done it, use that exact phrase, not just "worked with other teams". Then prove the most important skills inside your experience bullets; a skill that appears only in a list is less convincing than one shown in action. Include only skills you could answer questions about in an interview.

To see which keywords you are missing, paste the job description into the ATS resume checker. Aim for a match of 70% or more.

6. Education

List your degree, specialisation, institution and graduation year, most recent first. Freshers and recent graduates should add CGPA if it is 7.0/10 or above (or 70% and above), plus relevant coursework, honours or ranks. Experienced professionals can keep this section to one line per degree and move it below experience. Professional qualifications such as CA, CFA or an MBA belong here too.

7. Projects

Projects matter most for freshers, career changers and technical roles. Treat each one like a mini job: a name, the tools you used, and one or two bullets on what you built and what it achieved.

Before: Made an e-commerce website as final-year project.
After: Built a full-stack e-commerce site (React, Node.js, MongoDB) with cart, payments and admin dashboard; load-tested to [500] concurrent users.

8. Certifications and achievements

Add certifications that are relevant and recognised in your field, with the issuer and year. For achievements, choose ones with a clear measure: ranks, awards, competition results, scholarships, or recognition at work. "Ranked 2nd of 85 teams in a national case competition" is far stronger than "Participated in case competitions".

9. Resume length

Freshers and anyone with under five years of experience should aim for one page. Experienced professionals can use two. Senior executives may need up to three, but every line must still earn its place. If you are over, cut older roles to one or two bullets, remove skills you would not want to be tested on, and drop anything a recruiter for this job would not care about.

10. Proofread and export

Read your resume out loud, slowly. Check for typos, inconsistent dates ("Jan 2024" in one place, "01/2024" in another), mixed tenses, and spacing problems. Then ask a friend to read it for ten seconds and tell you what they remember: that is close to what a recruiter takes away.

Export a PDF with real, selectable text. Image-only PDFs and scanned documents cannot be read by most ATS. Keep a Word version for recruiters who ask for an editable file and a plain-text copy for forms that make you paste your resume. Name the file clearly, for example Priya-Sharma-Financial-Analyst.pdf.

11. Tailor it for every job

Sending the same resume to every opening is the most common reason good candidates are overlooked. Tailoring does not mean rewriting from scratch. For each application:

  1. Update your headline to match the job title.
  2. Adjust your summary to mention the two or three requirements the job stresses most.
  3. Reorder bullets so the most relevant achievements come first.
  4. Add missing keywords that are genuinely true for you, using the employer's wording.
  5. Pair your resume with a short, specific cover letter. Our cover letter generator builds one from your resume and the job description.

Keep a master resume with everything, and save a tailored version per application. Before sending, check that the file is ATS-safe using our ATS-friendly resume guide.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best format for a resume?

A single-column, reverse-chronological format with standard headings such as Experience, Education and Skills. It is easy for recruiters to scan and for applicant tracking systems to read.

How long should a resume be?

One page for freshers and anyone with under five years of experience, two pages for experienced professionals, and up to three for senior executives.

What should a resume bullet point include?

An action verb, what you did, and a measurable result such as a number, percentage, amount or time saved. The X-Y-Z formula (accomplished X, measured by Y, by doing Z) is a useful guide.

Should I write a different resume for each job?

Keep one master resume, then tailor the headline, summary, bullet order and keywords for each application. Small changes that mirror the job description make a big difference.
