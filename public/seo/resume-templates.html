Which resume template should you use?

A template should make your content easier to read, not compete with it. The right choice depends mostly on your industry and seniority. Here is who each ResumeForge Pro template suits.

Classic: banking, consulting, government and traditional corporates

Classic is the resume format most recruiters in conservative industries expect: a centred name block, simple section headings and a calm, formal look. If you are applying to banks, NBFCs, Big Four and strategy consulting firms, PSUs, government roles or long-established Indian and multinational corporates, Classic signals that you understand the setting. It is also the safest choice when you are not sure.

Modern: tech, product, startups and campus placements

Modern uses a clean sans-serif typeface and a contemporary layout. It looks current without being flashy, which suits software engineering, data, product management, design-adjacent roles, startups and campus placement drives. It is still single-column and ATS-safe, so you get the polish without the parsing risk.

Executive: directors, VPs and CXOs

Executive is built for senior profiles where scope matters as much as tasks: team size, P&L, revenue, markets and board-level outcomes. Its bold header band gives your name and headline real presence, and in Senior-Executive mode your Career Highlights sit near the top, so the scale of your work is obvious within seconds.

Compact: dense profiles

Compact uses side headings and tighter spacing so more fits on each page. It suits people with many short roles, consultants with long project lists, researchers with publications, or anyone who needs to bring a two-and-a-bit-page resume back to two pages. Compact is not a reason to add more text; it is a way to fit what already earns its place.

Why every template is single-column

Two-column and sidebar resumes look attractive in a design gallery, but they are risky in practice. Many applicant tracking systems read a page from left to right, line by line. A sidebar can get mixed into your work history, so a skill ends up in the middle of a job title, or your dates get separated from the roles they belong to. Tables, text boxes, icons for contact details and information placed in headers or footers cause similar problems.

Single-column layouts avoid all of that. The ATS reads your resume in the same order a recruiter does, top to bottom, and your sections land in the right fields. That is why all four templates use one column, standard headings such as Experience, Education and Skills, and a PDF export with real, selectable text. Learn more in our ATS-friendly resume guide.

Customise fonts, colours and sections

Every template can be adjusted without breaking its ATS safety:

  • Fonts: choose from clean, widely readable fonts suited to resumes.
  • Accent colour: pick a colour for headings and rules. Dark, muted colours print well and look professional.
  • Section order: move sections up or down. Freshers usually lead with Education and Projects; experienced professionals with Experience.
  • Hide sections: hide anything that does not help this application, without deleting it.

Your career mode (Fresher, Experienced Professional or Senior-Executive) sets a sensible default order, and the Recruiter Score adapts its checks to match.

What makes a resume format professional

Whatever template you choose, the same rules apply:

  • One page for freshers and anyone with under five years of experience; two pages for experienced professionals; up to three for executives.
  • Reverse-chronological order within each section, most recent first.
  • Consistent dates, such as "Jun 2023 – Present", in the same format everywhere.
  • Bullets that start with action verbs and include measurable results. Our action verbs list helps.
  • No photo, date of birth or marital status unless an employer specifically asks.
