Do you still need a cover letter?

Not every job application asks for one, and many recruiters go straight to the resume. But when a cover letter is requested, or when you are applying to a smaller company, changing careers, relocating or explaining a gap, a short, specific letter can be the reason a recruiter reads your resume more carefully. A generic letter does the opposite. That is why tailoring matters more than length.

The structure of a strong cover letter

Keep it to three or four short paragraphs, under one page. Each part has one job:

  1. Opening: the role you are applying for and one line on why you are a strong fit. Skip "I am writing to apply for…" and lead with something specific.
  2. Proof paragraph: one or two achievements from your resume that match the job's top requirements, with numbers where you have them.
  3. Why this company: one or two lines showing you understand what the team does and why the role interests you.
  4. Close: a confident, polite request for a conversation, and your name.
Generic opening: I am writing to express my interest in the Business Analyst position at your esteemed organisation.
Specific opening: I'm applying for the Business Analyst role on your payments team. In my current role I built the reporting that cut month-end reconciliation from [5] days to [2], and I'd like to bring that focus to your product.

Choose the right tone

Professional

Measured and formal. A good default for banks, consulting firms, government-linked organisations, large corporates and senior roles, where restraint reads as confidence.

Enthusiastic

Warmer and more energetic, while staying professional. Works well for startups, product companies, creative roles and freshers who want to show genuine interest in the company.

Short & direct

A few tight sentences covering the role, your best-matching achievement and a call to action. Ideal for email applications, LinkedIn messages and job portals with a small text box.

Tips for a cover letter that gets read

  • Address a person when you can. A name from the job post or LinkedIn beats "Dear Sir/Madam". "Dear Hiring Manager" is a safe fallback.
  • Mirror two or three keywords from the job description naturally. Check your resume against the same job with the ATS resume checker.
  • Do not repeat your resume. Pick the most relevant achievements and explain why they matter for this role.
  • Explain, do not apologise. If you are changing careers or returning after a break, give one positive line about it and move on.
  • Keep it under 300–350 words. Short letters are more likely to be read in full.
  • Match the file to the request. Download as PDF for uploads, Word if the employer asks for an editable file, or plain text to paste into a form.

Keep your resume and letter consistent

Because the letter is generated from your resume, update the resume first. If you have not polished it yet, start with the AI resume builder, pick an ATS-friendly template, then generate the letter.
