Why action verbs matter on a resume

Recruiters skim. When every bullet on your resume starts with "Responsible for" or "Worked on", nothing stands out and your achievements look like routine duties. A strong action verb does three things at once: it shows you did something, it tells the reader what kind of work it was, and it sets up a result.

Weak: Responsible for the onboarding process for new clients.
Strong: Redesigned client onboarding, cutting setup time from [10] days to [4] and reducing early cancellations by [X%].

The verb alone will not make a bullet great. Pair it with what you did and a measurable result, following the formula in our guide to writing a resume.

Weak phrases to replace

These phrases are the most common reasons bullets read as duties. Replace them with a verb that says what you actually did.

Worked onBuilt, Developed, Delivered, Designed
Helped with / Assisted inSupported, Contributed to, Co-led, Enabled
Was involved inCoordinated, Executed, Participated in (then say your part)
HandledResolved, Processed, Managed, Administered
Was in charge ofDirected, Headed, Supervised
MadeCreated, Produced, Designed, Built
Did / PerformedExecuted, Conducted, Completed, Carried out
Improved (with no detail)Increased, Reduced, Accelerated, plus the number
UsedApplied, Leveraged, Deployed, Implemented

Also cut the filler words that often follow: "various", "multiple", "different tasks", "day-to-day activities". Replace them with the actual number or the specific task.

Past or present tense?

The rule is simple:

  • Past tense for previous jobs, and for completed achievements in your current job: Launched, Reduced, Hired.
  • Present tense for ongoing responsibilities in your current job: Lead, Manage, Oversee.
Current role: Manage a ₹[X] crore annual marketing budget across 4 channels.
Same role, finished project: Launched a regional campaign that generated [1,200] qualified leads in [8] weeks.

Whatever you choose, be consistent within each role. Mixing "Managed" and "Manage" in neighbouring bullets for the same ongoing duty looks careless.

How to use action verbs well

  • Vary your verbs. Five bullets that all start with "Managed" blur together. Use the lists above to find a more precise word.
  • Match the verb to your real role. If you contributed to a project someone else led, say "Contributed to" or "Supported", then describe your part. Interviewers will ask.
  • Mirror the job description. If a posting asks someone to "analyse" and "forecast", and you have done that, use those verbs. It helps with ATS keyword matching too; check yours with the ATS resume checker.
  • Always follow with a result. Verb + what + measurable result. For freshers, results can be accuracy, users, rank or grade; see our resume for freshers examples.
  • Drop the pronouns. Write "Led a team of 6", not "I led a team of 6".

Frequently asked questions

What are action verbs on a resume?

Action verbs are strong, specific verbs such as Led, Built, Reduced or Negotiated that start each bullet point. They show what you did and set up a measurable result, instead of describing a duty.

Should resume bullets be in past or present tense?

Use past tense for previous jobs and for finished achievements in your current job. Use present tense only for ongoing responsibilities in your current role.

What should I write instead of "Responsible for"?

Use a verb that describes your actual role, such as Owned, Led, Managed or Ran, then add what you did and the result.

Can I use the same action verb more than once?

Occasionally, but try to vary them. Repeating the same verb across many bullets makes them blur together and hides the range of your work.
