ATS-friendly resume guide

How to make an ATS-friendly resume that software and recruiters can read

Applicant tracking systems do not reject resumes for fun, but they can misread them. This guide explains how ATS software parses your resume, the formatting rules that keep your information intact, and a checklist to run before every application.

How ATS software reads your resume

Most medium and large employers use an applicant tracking system to manage hiring. Common ones include Workday, Taleo, Greenhouse and Lever, and in India many recruiters also work through Naukri RMS. The details differ, but the basic process is similar:

  1. Extract the text. The system pulls the text out of your PDF or Word file. If the PDF is a scanned image, there may be no text to pull out.
  2. Parse it into fields. It looks for your name, email, phone, job titles, employers, dates, education and skills, usually by recognising standard headings and patterns.
  3. Store and search. Your parsed profile goes into a database. Recruiters search and filter it by keywords, titles, skills, location and experience.
  4. A person reviews. Shortlisted resumes are opened and read by a recruiter or hiring manager, often in the original format.

So an ATS-friendly resume has two goals: parse cleanly so your information lands in the right fields, and contain the right keywords so recruiters searching the database find you. Then it still has to impress a human.

Formatting do's

  • Use a single-column layout. Parsers read top to bottom, left to right. One column keeps your content in the order you intended.
  • Use standard section headings: Summary, Experience (or Work Experience), Education, Skills, Projects, Certifications. Creative headings like "My Journey" may not be recognised.
  • Submit a real-text PDF or a DOCX. If you can select and copy the text in your PDF, a parser can usually read it. Use DOCX if the employer asks for Word.
  • Use standard date formats such as "Jun 2022 – Present" or "06/2022 – 03/2024", consistently throughout.
  • Put job title, employer and dates on clear lines for each role, in the same order every time.
  • Use common fonts and simple round or square bullets.
  • Spell out acronyms once: "Search Engine Optimisation (SEO)" covers recruiters who search for either form.

Formatting don'ts

  • No tables or text boxes for core content. Some parsers skip them or read cells out of order.
  • No two-column or sidebar layouts for key information. A sidebar of skills can end up merged into your job titles.
  • No images, icons or charts carrying information. Skill bars and star ratings mean nothing to a parser, and icon-only phone or email labels can be lost.
  • Do not put key details in the header or footer. Some systems ignore those areas, so keep your name and contact details in the main body.
  • No scanned or image-only PDFs. Design-tool exports can produce these; always check you can select the text.
  • No unusual characters as bullets or separators, and no text hidden in white font. Recruiters see through keyword stuffing.

Keywords: mirror the job description

Recruiters search the ATS using words from the job description. If the posting says "financial modelling" and your resume only says "built forecasts in Excel", a search may miss you. Mirror the employer's exact wording where it is true for you.

Your wording: Made dashboards for the management team.
Job's wording: Built Power BI dashboards for senior leadership, tracking [12] KPIs across sales and operations.

Put important keywords in two places: the skills section, and the experience bullets that prove them. Do not paste the job description into your resume or list skills you lack; a human reads it next. Paste the job description into the ATS resume checker to see matched and missing keywords, and aim for 70% or more.

ATS-friendly resume checklist

Run through this before every application:

  • Single-column layout with no tables, text boxes or sidebars for key information
  • Name, phone, email, city and LinkedIn in the main body, not in a header
  • Standard headings: Summary, Experience, Education, Skills
  • Every role has a title, employer and start and end dates in one consistent format
  • Keywords from the job description used in the employer's exact wording, where true
  • Acronyms spelled out once
  • No skill bars, star ratings, photos or icons carrying information
  • PDF with selectable text (or DOCX if requested), with a clear file name
  • Plain-text copy ready for forms that ask you to paste your resume
  • Proofread for typos, consistent tense and consistent dates

ATS myths

"The ATS automatically rejects most resumes."

Many systems mainly store, organise and search applications; recruiters decide who moves forward, sometimes with knockout questions on the application form. The bigger risk is not a robot rejecting you but a recruiter never finding you because your resume was misread or lacked the right keywords.

"PDFs are not ATS-friendly."

Modern systems generally read PDFs with real text well. The problem is image-based PDFs. If the employer specifically asks for Word, send DOCX.

"You need a plain, ugly resume to pass the ATS."

No. A clean single-column layout with a professional font and a subtle accent colour parses perfectly well. What breaks parsing is structure (columns, tables, text boxes), not good typography.

"Stuffing keywords guarantees a shortlist."

Keyword lists without evidence, or hidden white text, may get you found but will not get you shortlisted, because a person reads the resume next. Use keywords naturally inside real achievements.

"One ATS-optimised resume works for every job."

Formatting can stay the same, but keywords change with every posting. Tailor your headline, summary and bullets each time. Our guide on how to write a resume covers tailoring step by step, and freshers can follow the fresher resume format.

Frequently asked questions

What is an ATS-friendly resume?

A resume that applicant tracking software can read correctly: single column, standard headings, real text rather than images, consistent dates, and keywords that match the job description.

Is PDF or Word better for ATS?

Both work if the PDF contains real, selectable text. Send a PDF by default and a DOCX when the employer asks for Word. Avoid scanned or image-only PDFs.

Do two-column resumes work with ATS?

Often not reliably. Some systems read across both columns line by line and mix up your content. A single-column layout is the safer choice.

How do I check if my resume is ATS-friendly?
